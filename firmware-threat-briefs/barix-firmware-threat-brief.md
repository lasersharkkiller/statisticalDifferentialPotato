# Barix AG Firmware Attack Surface & Detection Brief

**Scope & status (2026-10-27).** This vendor was on the "46 genuine OT vendors
still missing firmware" list and was acquired via **VirusTotal Intelligence**
during the 2026-10-26 acquire-and-analyze pass. The **behavioural differential
is complete** (see the section below). Full architecture enumeration, service
mapping, and CVE/advisory research have **not yet been performed** — unlike the
earlier CVE-researched briefs, this brief currently carries the VT behavioural
differential plus product/Purdue scoping only. Anything not stated below is "not
yet researched," not "absent."

**Census exposure:** 4 OT & Building-Systems devices across 3 states (per
[vendor-exposure-vs-coverage.md](vendor-exposure-vs-coverage.md)). A small
footprint, acquired for completeness.

**What Barix makes.** Barix AG is a Swiss manufacturer of **audio-over-IP**
endpoints: the *Exstreamer* (IP audio decoder/player), *Instreamer* (audio
encoder), *Annuncicom* (IP intercom / paging device with GPIO relay outputs and
contact inputs), and the IPAM audio module family. In practice these appear in
**IP public-address, mass-notification ("giant voice"), intercom, and
background-audio** systems. The higher-end devices run **embedded Linux** on
ARM/MIPS — which is why this vendor's baseline has a meaningful **ELF native-import
plane** — while discovery and configuration tooling (the Barix Discovery Tool /
firmware uploader) is Windows-side.

**Purdue layer mapping.** The audio endpoints are **field devices (L1-equivalent
by role)** — a direct physical-world interface (audio output, GPIO relay/contact
I/O) with no intervening controller, the same role framing used for Axis cameras
and the A1001 door controller. The Windows-side discovery/config tooling is
management-plane (**L2/L3**). An Annuncicom driving relay outputs on a
notification circuit is the audio analog of a door-strike relay: compromise is a
**physical-effect** concern (spoofed or suppressed announcements), not only a
data concern.

**Data-quality note.** The VT-Intelligence acquisition for Barix is
heterogeneous — embedded-Linux firmware images alongside ancillary files. The
single-engine `trojan.`-labelled ELF samples that appear in
`output/new-vendor-vt-detections.csv` for this vendor are consistent with the
project-wide finding that **single-engine detections on embedded firmware are
false-positive noise**, not confirmed malware; none reached the >=5-engine bar
used for the impersonation split. Treat the baseline as sound but
noise-bearing until a dedicated acquisition review is done.
